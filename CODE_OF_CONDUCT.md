# PRUDENCE Code of Conduct

## Purpose

PRUDENCE welcomes people who want to develop, test, challenge, and improve a shared foundation for consequential decisions. Participants bring different disciplines, institutional perspectives, technical abilities, and experiences. This Code establishes the standards for working together respectfully and responsibly.

It applies equally to contributors, users, reviewers, maintainers, and Signal representatives.

## Scope

This Code applies in official PRUDENCE repositories, issue trackers, pull requests, discussion channels, meetings, and project events. It also applies when someone officially represents PRUDENCE.

Project-related private communications are covered when they involve harassment, intimidation, retaliation, or other conduct that affects participation in the community.

## Expected conduct

Participants are expected to:

- Treat others respectfully, regardless of background, identity, disability, nationality, religion, professional status, or level of experience.
- Address ideas, evidence, and implementation choices on their merits.
- Explain concerns clearly and offer examples or supporting reasoning where possible.
- Welcome questions, acknowledge uncertainty, and correct errors.
- Respect contributors' time, authorship, and attribution.
- Disclose relevant affiliations or commercial interests when advocating a proposal, product, or implementation.
- Follow contribution procedures and use synthetic or properly authorized material.

Strong disagreement, criticism of Signal or PRUDENCE, and proposals that challenge existing decisions are welcome when expressed constructively. Participants are not required to agree with maintainers to remain in good standing.

## Unacceptable conduct

Unacceptable conduct includes:

- Harassment, discrimination, threats, stalking, intimidation, or targeted humiliation.
- Sexual harassment, unwelcome sexual attention, or sexualized content unrelated to legitimate project discussion.
- Publishing private information or confidential records without authorization.
- Impersonating another person or misrepresenting affiliation, authority, authorship, or project endorsement.
- Repeated unwanted contact after a request to stop.
- Deliberate disruption, spam, or repeated promotional activity that interferes with project work.
- Retaliation against someone for reporting a concern or participating in its review.
- Knowingly false reports or fabricated evidence.

A report that cannot be substantiated is not, by itself, a false report.

## Reporting a concern

Send reports privately to **admin@signalprivatemarkets.com**.

Reports should distinguish what happened, the evidence available, the reporter's interpretation, and the response requested. Use the following structure where possible:

1. **Context and participants.** Identify the project space, approximate date and time, people involved, and whether you experienced or witnessed the conduct.
2. **Conduct reported.** Describe the specific actions or communications. Identify the relevant provision of this Code if you can.
3. **Evidence.** Provide relevant links, messages, or other supporting material you are authorized to share. Distinguish firsthand observations from information received from others, and identify gaps or uncertainty.
4. **Impact and immediate concerns.** Explain how the conduct affected participation and whether it is continuing or requires an immediate protective response.
5. **Requested response.** State what action or resolution you seek and why.
6. **Contact and disclosure preferences.** Explain how you may be contacted and any concerns about sharing your identity or supporting material.

This structure supports review; it is not a condition for receiving a report. Incomplete or anonymous reports will be considered, although limited information may constrain the response. Do not post sensitive details in public issues or discussions.

## Review, decision, and follow-through

Signal administers this Code through **admin@signalprivatemarkets.com**. No alternate reviewer is currently designated.

Each report will have a private review record documenting:

- The concern, available evidence, and material uncertainties.
- Who is responsible for review and has authority to decide the response.
- Any involvement or conflict of interest affecting that reviewer.
- The assessment against this Code, with observations distinguished from conclusions.
- The decision, its reasons, and any protective or corrective action.
- Who will carry out the action, its duration where applicable, and its observed outcome.
- Any reconsideration and the reasons for changing or maintaining the decision.

The person whose conduct is questioned will have an opportunity to respond when appropriate. Immediate protective measures may be taken while review continues.

If a report concerns the person administering this Code, that involvement must be recorded and disclosed to the reporter. Signal must explain how the concern will be handled and any limitation on impartial review. Review by the person concerned will not be represented as independent.

Report information will be shared only as reasonably necessary to assess the incident, protect participants, or meet applicable obligations. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised. Affected participants will receive an appropriate explanation of the outcome without unnecessary disclosure of another person's private information.

## Responses and reconsideration

Responses will reflect the severity, impact, repetition, and circumstances of the conduct. They may include:

- A request to correct behavior or remove inappropriate material.
- A written warning or defined limits on participation.
- Temporary suspension from project spaces.
- Permanent exclusion for serious or repeated misconduct.

Serious incidents may warrant immediate suspension or exclusion without a prior warning. Reviewers will document the reasons for their decisions and communicate them to affected participants as appropriate.

A participant may request reconsideration privately, identifying relevant new information or a procedural concern. Where practicable, a reviewer who did not make the original decision will assess the request.

## Relationship to project governance

This Code governs conduct and participation. Technical proposals and release decisions remain subject to [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md); contributions remain subject to [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

Conduct decisions do not transfer contributor ownership or revoke rights already granted under the Apache License 2.0. Commercial competition, independent implementations, and good-faith criticism are not violations of this Code.
